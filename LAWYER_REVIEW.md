Open questions and assumptions in the draft Privacy Policy and Terms of Service. Please check each before the documents are finalised.

## Points to review

1. **Written for launch.** The documents describe the app as it will work with a backend. Today all data stays on the user's phone, so the hosting provider, backups and server-side security described in the policy aren't in use yet.
2. **Data beyond the original brief.** The privacy policy also covers four things the app handles:
   - the saved Face ID login (email and password in the iPhone's Keychain, on the device only);
   - Car of the Month entries (a car photo and caption) and votes;
   - "my car is missing from the list" reports;
   - feature-idea votes.
3. **DSA trader information (Art. 30).** Business accounts must provide name, address, phone number, email, trade register number and tax number, and self-certify that they only offer products that comply with EU law. The app collects these fields at registration and in Account settings (added alongside this list), but has **no self-certification step yet**. The terms also say we *check* the details; confirm what checking is required, and the self-certification wording and where the app should ask for it.
4. **Parental consent.** The terms require parental or guardian consent for buyers and sellers under 18, on top of the 16+ minimum age.
5. **Retention periods.** The 2-year retention for support emails and for abuse reports is a suggestion.
6. **Consumer dispute resolution.** The terms point consumers to the conciliation board (békéltető testület). The EU online dispute resolution platform shut down in July 2025, so it isn't mentioned.
7. **Menu paths in the Hungarian pages.** ~~The app's interface is English-only for now, so the Hungarian texts quote the English menu paths.~~ Resolved: the app is now in Hungarian and English (it follows the App language setting), so the Hungarian privacy policy and terms quote the Hungarian menus (e.g. "Profil → Beállítások → Fiók → Fiók törlése", confirmed by typing TÖRLÉS, or DELETE with the app in English).
8. **Not indexed while in draft.** Every page asks search engines not to index it. Remove the tag from each page when the documents are final.
9. **6-month retention of trader information (DSA Art. 30(5)).** When a Business account ends (the account is deleted, or switched to Personal), the app's backend copies the trader information to a separate archive that no user can read, and deletes it automatically after 6 months. Both privacy policies now say so (section 5, and section 6 for deletion). Please confirm:
   - **What's archived.** Only the Art. 30(1) points the app collects: (a) name, address, phone number and email, (d) trade register number, (e) the self-certification. The **tax number** isn't in Art. 30, so it's deleted straight away (point 3 above lists it among the DSA fields; please check that too).
   - **When the 6 months start.** We count from account deletion or the switch to Personal. Is that "the end of the contractual relationship"?
   - **Whether we must keep it at all.** Art. 29 exempts platforms that are micro or small enterprises from Art. 30. We keep the information anyway, as if the exemption didn't apply. Is that the right call, and is the legal basis still "legal obligation" (Art. 6(1)(c)) if we're exempt?
   - **Disclosure.** The archive is only disclosed where the law requires (Art. 30(6)). There's no in-app access.
   - **Not collected yet:** (b) an ID document and (c) payment account details. Art. 30(1) requires both before a trader may sell.
10. **Listing photos and videos, and profile photos, are publicly reachable by link.** They're stored with our hosting provider as files with long random web addresses (a "public bucket"). Anyone who has a file's address can open it without signing in; the addresses can't be guessed and aren't listed anywhere, but a shared link works for whoever receives it. The rest of the data (profiles, listings, chats) stays visible to signed-in users only. We chose this because the files load much faster and are cached; the alternative is links that expire after a short time. Both privacy policies now say so (section 4). Please confirm:
    - **Whether this is acceptable** for photos people upload themselves, given that they may show number plates, faces or the inside of someone's home or garage. Should the app warn about that when adding photos, or should number plates be blurred?
    - **Whether section 4 explains it clearly enough**, and whether it also belongs under section 9 (Security).
11. **30-day deletion of sold listings' photos and videos.** When a seller marks a listing as sold, other users can still see it for 30 days (for example in their saved listings), with its photos and videos. After 30 days a daily job deletes the photos and videos (so in practice within 30–31 days), and the listing is then visible only to the seller, without them. Photos removed from a listing, deleted listings and replaced profile photos are deleted at once, or by the same daily job within 2 days. Both privacy policies now list these periods (section 5). Please confirm:
    - **The 30 days**, and whether the policy should say "within 31 days" rather than "30 days after the sale".
    - **Whether the sold listing's text** (title, description, price, location) should also be deleted at some point rather than kept until the seller deletes it or their account. Today it stays.
12. **Saved listings are now stored on the server.** Until now, the listings a user saved (the heart) were kept only on their phone. They're now stored with our hosting provider: which listing, when it was saved, the price at that moment, and the lower price we last told the user about (so each price drop is notified once). Only the user can see their saved listings; sellers aren't told who saved theirs. The record is kept until the user removes it, the seller deletes the listing, or the user deletes their account; a listing sold more than 30 days ago no longer shows in Saved, but the record stays until then. Both privacy policies now cover this (sections 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6). Please confirm:
    - **The legal basis**, performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)), for keeping the saved price to send price-drop notifications the user turned on.
    - **Whether the saved record of a listing sold over 30 days ago** should be deleted rather than kept until one of the events above.
13. **Chat messages, offers and blocks are now stored on the server.** Until now, chats and offers were kept only on the user's phone. Chats with other users (one conversation per listing, between its buyer and seller) are now stored with our hosting provider: the messages, offers and counter-offers with their amount, optional message and status, when each was sent, and when each side last opened the conversation (shown to the other side as "Seen"). Whether someone is typing is passed on live and not stored. The list of users someone blocked is stored too. Only the two people in a conversation can read it; only the blocker sees their block list (the blocked person isn't told, but can't message them). Notifications about new messages and offers are created on the phone while the app is open (no push service yet). Both privacy policies now cover this (sections 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6). Please confirm:
    - **Retention.** A conversation is deleted when either person deletes their account, and automatically 2 years after its last message or offer answer. Is 2 years appropriate (e.g. as evidence of what was agreed in a sale), or should it be shorter or longer? There's no way yet to delete a single conversation yourself; is one needed?
    - **Deletion for both sides.** When one person deletes their account, the messages and offers of their conversations are deleted for the other person too, who then sees an empty conversation with a "Deleted user". The previous draft said the other person keeps their own copy. Is deleting the other side's copy (including what *they* wrote) acceptable, or should their own messages stay?
    - **Access by us.** The policy says chats are visible only to the two participants. Technically, our administrators can access the database (for example to handle a report or a legal request). Should the policy say when we may look at a conversation (reports of illegal content, security, legal obligations), and is the legal basis for that legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) or legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c))?
    - **The legal basis** for chats, offers and blocks: performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)).
    - **Offers aren't binding.** An accepted offer only says the two sides agreed on a price; the app doesn't take payment or mark the listing sold. Should the Terms say that explicitly?
14. **Reports are stored, and reported chats are kept after an account is deleted.** Reports of users, listings and chats were only emails; they're now also stored on the server (reporter, what was reported, reason, details, time, open/closed). Only we can read them; users can only create them. They're deleted 2 years after they were made. When an account is deleted, its conversations are normally deleted for both sides (point 13), **except** conversations tied to an open report: the report is about that conversation, about either participant, or about the conversation's listing. Those are kept with their messages and offers, hidden from both users and accessible only to us, until the report is closed or at most 6 months after the deletion, and then deleted. Both privacy policies now say so (sections 2, 5 and 6). Please confirm:
    - **The legal basis** for keeping a deleted user's messages for a report: legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)), or legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c)) where the DSA requires us to handle notices (Art. 16) and keep what's needed for complaints (Art. 20)? Does this override the right to erasure (Art. 17(3)(e), establishment or defence of legal claims)?
    - **The 6-month maximum.** Is it long enough to handle a report (and a possible complaint, which the DSA allows for 6 months after a decision, Art. 20(1)), or should it run from our decision rather than from the deletion?
    - **The scope.** A report about *either* participant holds every conversation of the deleted user with them, also when the report is about something else. Is that proportionate, or should only the reported conversation (or the reported person's chats) be kept?
    - **Telling the other person.** The held conversation simply disappears from the other person's list, with no explanation. Should the app tell them it's kept for a report?
    - **The 2 years for reports** (also point 5), now that it's enforced automatically, and whether a closed report's details should be deleted sooner.
15. **Moderation: decisions on reports, removed listings, suspended accounts, appeals.** Reports are now decided in the app by our moderators (people we designate; the role can only be given directly in the database). Each decision is one of: **no action**, **remove the listing**, or **suspend the account**, always with a written reason, and we store who decided and when with the report (kept with the report for 2 years). A removed listing is hidden from everyone but its seller, who sees it as "Removed" with the reason; it can't be edited or relisted and is deleted 6 months after removal. A suspended user can't post or edit listings, send messages, or make or answer offers, and their listings are hidden from others; they can still log in, see their data, and delete their account. The suspension lasts until we lift it (no fixed end). The reporter sees the outcome (not the reason) under Profile → Settings → My reports. Both the affected user and the reporter are told they can appeal by email to motorverse@gmail.com within 6 months. Moderators can read a reported conversation only while its report is open. The privacy policies (sections 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6) and the terms (sections 7 and 8) now cover this. Please confirm:
    - **Statement of reasons (DSA Art. 17).** The affected user sees the decision and our free-text reason in the app, plus the appeal route. Art. 17(3) also asks for the facts relied on, whether automated means were used, the legal or contractual ground, and the redress options (internal complaint, out-of-court settlement, court). Is an in-app message enough, or must we also send it by email, and should the app use a fixed template (ground + facts + redress) rather than free text? Should we show the reason when a user is suspended for something that isn't the listing they can see?
    - **Telling the reporter (Art. 16(5)).** The reporter sees only the outcome ("listing removed", "user suspended", "no action"), not our reasoning, to protect the reported user. Is that enough, and must they also be told about out-of-court dispute settlement?
    - **Appeals (Art. 20).** Appeals are by email only, within 6 months of the decision, reviewed by a person; restoring a listing or lifting a suspension is done by us manually. Is email an acceptable "internal complaint-handling system" (Art. 20(3) asks for an easy-to-access, user-friendly electronic system), or do we need an in-app form? Does the 6 months run from the decision or from when the user is told?
    - **Removed listings deleted after 6 months.** We delete a removed listing (with its photos) 6 months after removal, the appeal window. Is that right, or should we keep it until a pending appeal is closed, or longer as evidence?
    - **Indefinite suspensions (Art. 23).** Suspensions have no end date. Art. 23(1) speaks of suspension "for a reasonable period of time" after a prior warning, for frequent manifestly illegal content. Do we need a warning step, a fixed duration, or a review date? The terms (section 8) say we first warn, then suspend: the app has no warning step yet.
    - **Suspended users' listings are hidden**, including their recently sold ones, without telling other users why. Is that proportionate? Should existing conversations with a suspended user show a note to the other person?
    - **Moderators reading reported conversations.** Access is limited to conversations named by an open report, and ends when the report is closed. Is the legal basis (legal obligation under DSA Art. 16, plus legitimate interest) right, and is section 4 of the privacy policy clear enough? This also answers the "Access by us" question in point 13.
    - **Transparency database (Art. 24(5)).** Statements of reasons must be submitted to the Commission's DSA Transparency Database. Micro and small enterprises are exempt from Art. 24 except 24(3) (Art. 19). Please confirm we're exempt, and whether we still need the yearly transparency report (Art. 15, also exempt for micro/small enterprises).
    - **Record of who decided.** We store which moderator decided. If the moderator later deletes their account, the decision stays without their id. Is that acceptable, and should the decision record name a person at all?
16. **Reviews are stored on the server, and only buyers who dealt with a seller can write them.** Until now reviews, replies and "helpful" votes were kept only on the user's phone (so nobody else saw them). They're now stored with our hosting provider: the review (stars, text, when it was written and last edited), the seller's reply, and each user's up/down "helpful" vote (private; others see only counts). Reviews are visible to signed-in users with the reviewer's name and photo; the seller's average and count are calculated from the reviews shown. **Who can review:** only a user with a chat about one of the seller's listings in which the seller replied at least once, or with an accepted offer from that seller; never yourself, not a user you blocked or who blocked you, not while suspended. The check runs on our servers on the existing chats and offers; nothing extra is stored. One review per seller, editable and deletable; one reply per review by the seller; reviews and replies can be reported and removed by moderation (point 15), shown to the author with the reason, kept hidden for 6 months for an appeal, then deleted. When either account is deleted, the reviews by and about it go too (with replies and votes), except reported ones, held hidden until the report closes or at most 6 months (as for chats, point 14). Demo sellers' reviews stay on the phone. The privacy policies (sections 2, 4, 5 and 6) and the terms (sections 6, 7 and 8) now cover this. Please confirm:
    - **Consumer-law rules on reviews (Omnibus Directive, Hungarian Fttv. / UCPD Annex I points 23b–23c).** A platform that gives access to consumer reviews must say whether and how it ensures they come from consumers who actually used or bought the product. Our check is "the seller replied in a chat or accepted an offer", not a completed purchase (the app doesn't handle payment). Is the explanation in the terms (section 6) enough, is it clear and prominent enough, and does it need to say that a chat reply doesn't prove a purchase?
    - **Deleting reviews about a seller when the seller deletes their account**, and deleting a reviewer's reviews when they delete theirs (so averages can change). Is that acceptable, or should reviews survive (anonymised) as useful information for other buyers? For business sellers, is there any obligation to keep reviews?
    - **Removing reviews and the average.** A removed review drops out of the average. Under the consumer rules, platforms mustn't misrepresent reviews (e.g. by suppressing negative ones). Our removals are only for rule or law breaches after a report, by a person, with a reason. Is that enough, and should the terms list the removal grounds more precisely?
    - **Legal basis**: performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)) for reviews, replies and votes, and for using chats and offers to check eligibility (Art. 6(1)(f), legitimate interest in genuine reviews, may fit the check better). Which one?
    - **Showing the reviewer's name and photo** to every signed-in user. Should reviewers be able to post under a shorter name (e.g. first name and initial)?
    - **Blocks.** Existing reviews stay visible after a block, but the reviewer can't edit them while the block lasts; the seller can still reply. Is that fair to both sides?
    - **Retention of 6 months for removed reviews**, same questions as for removed listings (point 15).
